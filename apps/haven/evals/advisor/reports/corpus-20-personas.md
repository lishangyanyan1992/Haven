## bridge-b2-pending-offer

**Q.** I filed B-2 and now I have an offer. Do I wait for the B-2 to be approved first?

Short answer: No — you do not necessarily have to wait for the B‑2 change‑of‑status (COS) to be approved before your new employer files an H‑1B petition. What matters is that any H‑1B petition seeking a change of status is filed while you are still in an authorized period of stay.

Key points and steps
- Governing rule: 8 CFR 248.1 requires a change‑of‑status application to be filed while the applicant is still in an authorized period; a late filing can lead to denial except in very narrow discretionary circumstances. (See 8 CFR 248.1(b).)
- Who files: An H‑1B petition must be employer‑filed (8 CFR 248.3). Your employer can file an H‑1B petition requesting change of status to H‑1B even if your B‑2 COS is still pending — provided the H‑1B is filed while you remain in an authorized period.
- Work authorization: Do not begin working until the H‑1B petition/change‑of‑status is approved (or you have another valid work authorization). Receipt notices do not by themselves authorize employment.
- Timing risks to check now:
  - Was the B‑2 COS filed before your prior authorized status expired (or within the applicable grace period)? If not, USCIS may treat your B‑2 filing as untimely and that can affect any later COS request.
  - If the H‑1B petition requests change of status but USCIS finds you didn’t maintain status, the petition’s change‑of‑status portion could be denied even if the underlying petition is approvable (or the approval may be for consular processing instead).
  - B‑2 used as a “bridge” can trigger RFEs about intent; keep documentation showing maintenance of status and your timeline.
- Practical steps for you and the employer:
  - Confirm the B‑2 COS filing date and your current I‑94/status period.
  - Ask the employer to file the H‑1B petition and specifically request change of status (if you want U.S. change rather than consular processing).
  - Consider premium processing and have counsel prepare to respond to possible RFEs about status/intent.
  - Don’t travel outside the U.S. while a COS is pending (travel generally abandons the COS application).
- Community note: There are anecdotal cases where people filed B‑2 as a bridge, found an employer, and the employer’s H‑1B COS was approved while the B‑2 was pending — but these are individual stories and not a guarantee for your case.

## bridge-h4-return

**Q.** Can I go to H-4 through my wife and then back to H-1B without the lottery?

Short answer: Yes — but only with conditions. You can change from H‑1B to H‑4 (you file the change of status) and later return to H‑1B (your employer must file an H‑1B petition). Whether you need to go through the H‑1B lottery again depends on your prior H‑1B cap history and whether you maintain lawful status during the change.

Key points and required actions
- Change to H‑4 is an individual change-of-status application and must be filed while you are maintaining lawful nonimmigrant status (8 CFR 248.3; 8 CFR 248.1). Do not let your H‑1B status expire before filing.  
  Source: 8 CFR 248.1, 248.3 (links below).
- Coming back to H‑1B requires an employer-filed H‑1B petition. That petition must be filed by the prospective employer; an employee cannot self-file. (8 CFR 248.3)
- Lottery requirement: if you were previously counted against the H‑1B cap, you are generally eligible to be counted as cap‑exempt when an employer later petitions for you — meaning no new lottery — but if you were never cap‑counted, a new cap filing (and lottery if cap-subject) would be needed. Confirm your prior cap-counted status with counsel or the employer/USCIS records.
- Maintain status when you apply to change to H‑4. 8 CFR 248.1(b) warns change-of-status may be denied if you failed to maintain the prior status or filed after your authorized stay expired.
- Six‑year H‑1B limit and your I-140: because you noted an approved I‑140 and layoff, there are additional rules that can affect extensions beyond the six‑year limit or eligibility to return without a lottery — those are case-specific and should be reviewed with an immigration attorney before making the move.
- Work while on H‑4: H‑4 does not by itself grant work authorization unless you separately qualify and obtain authorization (H‑4 EAD in limited situations). Do not assume you may work while on H‑4 without confirmed EAD.

Next steps I recommend now
1. Confirm whether your wife is the H‑1B principal (i.e., that she can sponsor you as H‑4) and the dates of her H‑1B validity.  
2. If you still have valid H‑1B status / I‑94, file the H‑4 change-of-status before that status expires.  
3. Before changing, ask prospective H‑1B employers whether they will file a cap‑exempt H‑1B petition when you seek to return (and confirm your prior cap‑count).  
4. Consult an immigration attorney to review your six‑year cap exposure, layoff timing/grace-period issues, and implications of your approved I‑140.

Official references
- 8 CFR 248.1 (change of nonimmigrant classification; filing while maintaining status; discretionary late filing): https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-248/section-248.1  
- 8 CFR 248.3 (who files change-of-status applications; individuals file for dependents such as H‑4): https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-248/section-248.3
